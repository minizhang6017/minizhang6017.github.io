PantryPal

Designing a Smarter Way to Manage Food at Home

New Product Development Capstone · Product Strategy · User Research · UI/UX Design · 2025

A connected way to save money, reduce food waste, and simplify meal planning.

PantryPal is a mobile pantry-management concept designed to help users track food inventory, reduce waste, manage grocery spending, and simplify meal planning.

01

The Challenge

Our research found that users often struggle with keeping track of food at home, managing expiration dates, and planning meals efficiently. Existing solutions addressed parts of the problem, but many were fragmented, overly complex, or dependent on expensive smart-home technology.

02

Methods

  • Market Research: Reviewed existing pantry, meal-planning, and smart-kitchen solutions to identify market gaps and competitive opportunities.
  • User Research: Used exploratory research, lead-user interviews, concept-engineering interviews, and a survey with 32 respondents to understand grocery habits and pain points.
  • Feature Prioritization: Applied opportunity scoring and trade-off analysis to identify high-value user needs and determine which features to prioritize.
  • UI/UX Design: Translated research insights into mobile interface concepts for inventory management, product scanning, spending tracking, and item-level information.
  • Product Validation: Evaluated adoption barriers, differentiation, and business viability before making a launch recommendation.
03

The Solution

PantryPal brings food management into one connected experience:

  • AI-assisted product scanning
  • Inventory and expiration tracking
  • Flexible quantity recording
  • Recipe and dietary recommendations
  • Local price comparison
  • Grocery spending and budget tracking
Three Product Goals

Save money. Reduce food waste. Adapt to existing habits and routines.

04

Key Product Insight

Useful ≠
Launch-Ready

The final evaluation revealed important risks around behavioural change, manual-input fatigue, limited differentiation, and profitability.

Rather than recommending an immediate launch, the project concluded that PantryPal required further validation and stronger differentiation before moving forward.

05

Key Takeaway

The project demonstrated how product development moves from market and user research to prioritization, design, and validation.

It reinforced the importance of balancing user needs, usability, differentiation, and business viability when evaluating a new product concept.
